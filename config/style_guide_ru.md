# Editorial style guide (RU output)

This file captures practical rules for literary editing of Russian translations.

## Tone and voice
- Prefer clarity and precision over excessive imagery.
- Avoid bureaucratic clichés and Anglicisms unless terminology requires them.
- Preserve the author’s rhythm and intent while using natural Russian syntax.

## Punctuation and formatting
- Use em dashes (—) for asides and authorial insertions.
- Use «ёлочки» for quotes and „лапки“ for nested quotes.
- Spell out numbers up to ten unless the context is technical.

## Translation decisions
- Preserve specialized terminology according to the glossary.
- When unsure, keep the exact meaning of the original and refine during editing.

## Commenting style
- For debatable phrasing, add notes in the QA report, not in the final text.

## Natural Russian quality standard (required)
- Apply the full guide in [docs/russian_translation_quality.md](../docs/russian_translation_quality.md) and review [editorial regression cases](../examples/russian_quality_regressions.md).
- Preserve semantic fidelity **and** native Russian expression. Do not reproduce English syntax or business idioms word for word.
- Do not add unsupported hedges such as «похоже» or «вероятно», but preserve uncertainty when the author actually expresses it.
- Do not invent motives, intentions, certainty, praise, or explanations in the name of stylistic improvement.
- Prefer a direct, concise Russian construction when appropriate. Sentence splitting is allowed if facts, modality and emphasis survive.
- Borrowed words are acceptable when established in the domain; do not confuse a loanword with a syntactic calque.
- Human-approved examples override generic stylistic preferences **in their context**. They are not universal string replacements.
